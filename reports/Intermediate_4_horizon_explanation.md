My model does not beat the naive forecast (“tomorrow will be the same as today”) at any horizon, and it gets relatively worse the further ahead it looks. At 1 day its error is about the same as naive ($1.49 vs $1.48), but at 63 days it is about a third larger ($17.23 vs $12.91), and its out-of-sample R² falls from -0.02 to -0.41. Stock prices are mostly noise, and over longer horizons that noise adds up, so a model with many predictors fits patterns that do not repeat, while the naive forecast makes no assumptions that can be wrong. The 10%-90% range is also overconfident at long horizons: it contained 80% of outcomes at 1 day but only 66.5% at 63 days.
